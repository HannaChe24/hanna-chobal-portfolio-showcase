THE CHALLENGE

BRINGING A WEB TRADING PLATFORM TO MOBILE

The team was redesigning a trading platform that brokers could offer under their own brand. There was no mobile version yet. The challenge was to preserve the capabilities of the web product while organising market overview, instrument research and order entry into a clear sequence on a small screen.

During the project, the white-label platform operated under a different brand. This design became the foundation for the product now marketed as Xcite.

The primary audience was beginner traders gaining their first trading experience. The mobile version needed to help them find an instrument, explore its information and configure their first order, with additional parameters available when needed.

APPROACH

REVEAL DETAILS WHEN THEY ARE NEEDED

On the web, several information panels are visible at once. For mobile, I reorganised the main overview sections into a single vertical feed and adapted the information density to a narrow screen. Instrument research and order setup open separately.

The order form brings essential parameters together, with additional settings available in context. This helps beginners start with the essentials and access further options when needed.

MY ROLE · MOBILE UX

FROM SHARED COMPONENTS TO MOBILE FLOWS

I owned the design of the first mobile version: screen structure, navigation and interactions. I defined how to regroup the web panels for a phone and which order parameters to show immediately or on demand. I worked directly with the Product Owner, a backend developer and a frontend developer.

Alongside the mobile work, I created and maintained the shared design system used by the development team. I adapted its components for mobile flows while maintaining consistency with the web platform and other product interfaces.

WEB AND MOBILE

REORGANISING DATA FOR A MOBILE SCREEN

On the web, the account overview brings together trades, a watchlist, top movers and upcoming events. A sidebar provides access to the other sections. The larger screen allows several sources of information to be viewed simultaneously.

What I preserved. Trades, the watchlist, top movers and upcoming events remain in the mobile overview. Users can browse the main sections on one screen by scrolling through the feed.

What I reorganised. The web panels are arranged in a single column. In the watchlist and top movers, a compact chart replaces the Low/High range. The wide events table becomes cards showing the time, instrument, event and impact; forecast and previous-value columns are omitted from the mobile overview.

ORDER ENTRY: FROM A SIDE PANEL TO A DEDICATED SCREEN

On the web, the buy form opens to the right of the chart, keeping market context and order parameters visible together. On mobile, the form gets a dedicated screen so fields and controls remain comfortable to use.

What is revealed on demand. The order form groups essential parameters together, with additional settings such as Stop Loss and Take Profit available in context.

The trade-off: Fewer sections are visible at once, so the overview requires scrolling. Event cards show fewer fields than the web table. In return, information remains readable without squeezing multiple columns onto a phone.
